# Brief: what should the first three seconds be?

## Who it is for

People who already play casual puzzle or word games on their phone, seeing this between
two other things. They give an ad a second or two before deciding. (This is my assumption
about the audience, not data.)

## What I saw in the market

Meta's Ad Library, Turkey, keyword "tile", sorted by impressions, October 2026. Among the
game ads on the first page:

- most are videos of about thirty seconds;
- the copy is either a challenge ("Only 1% can clear level 100", "Only 1% users can pass
  lv 3") or a promise of calm ("Pure relaxing puzzle fun", "Tile matching joy awaits").

I read the listings; I did not watch every video, so I can't say what their first seconds
look like.

## What I want to learn

The three variants we have test the tone of the promise: challenge (duel), pressure
(comeback) and calm (relax). This batch tests something else: **what the viewer sees and
does first.** For example:

- a round that is nearly over when it starts, so the first word decides it;
- an opponent who is slightly ahead rather than far ahead;
- no headline question at all, only an instruction;
- a night look, to stand out in a feed that is mostly bright.

These are examples, not the list. Bring better ones.

## Fixed

- The brand stays WORD TILE DUEL and the opponent stays a person with a short first name.
- Keep the store button's label to PLAY NOW or PLAY FREE.
- Do not touch `botMercy`.

Three.
